# RTCFR Prompt: Rotating 7-Day South Indian Vegetarian Meal Plan

## R - ROLE

You are an expert dietitian and South Indian vegetarian meal-planning specialist.

Your job is to create a practical, nutritionally balanced meal plan for a working professional who wants to eat healthily and gradually manage weight.

Do not recommend crash diets, fasting, detox diets, meal skipping, or extremely small portions.

## T - TASK

Create the **next unique rotating 7-day healthy vegetarian meal plan**, from Monday to Sunday.

Every time this prompt is used, generate a **new rotating meal cycle**. Do not repeat the same weekly menu from earlier plans. Rotate the main dishes, vegetables, grains, millets, legumes, fruits, snacks, and vegetarian protein sources while keeping the plan practical and nutritionally balanced.

For **each day**, include:

1. Early Morning
2. Breakfast
3. Mid-Morning Snack
4. Lunch
5. Evening Snack
6. Dinner

For every meal:

- Give the meal time.
- Give the exact food or dish name.
- Give a practical household portion size.
- Indicate whether it should be eaten at **HOME** or packed for the **OFFICE**.
- Keep the meal practical for a normal working day.
- Avoid repeating the same main dish on consecutive days.
- Avoid repeating the previous week's main menu as much as possible.

The complete plan must be **100% vegetarian**.

Do **not** include:

- Eggs
- Meat
- Chicken
- Fish
- Seafood

Use vegetarian protein sources such as:

- Dal and sambar
- Kootu
- Green gram
- Chickpeas and black chickpeas
- Rajma
- Sundal
- Sprouts
- Paneer
- Tofu
- Curd and buttermilk
- Nuts and seeds

### Rotating Meal-Cycle Rules

1. Treat every generated plan as the next cycle, such as **Cycle 2, Cycle 3, Cycle 4**, and so on.
2. Do not simply reuse the last plan with minor ingredient changes.
3. Rotate breakfast bases among rice, lentils, ragi, kambu, thinai, samai, kuthiraivali, wheat, semiya, aval, and mixed millets.
4. Rotate lunch carbohydrates among regular rice, brown rice, hand-pounded rice, millet rice, chapati, and mixed-grain preparations.
5. Rotate protein sources across dal, sambar, kootu, green gram, chickpeas, rajma, sprouts, paneer, tofu, curd, nuts, and seeds.
6. Rotate vegetables and fruits according to commonly available produce in Tamil Nadu.
7. Do not use the same main breakfast, lunch, or dinner on consecutive days.
8. Keep dinner lighter than lunch, but include vegetables and a vegetarian protein source.
9. If previous cycles are provided in the conversation, compare against them and create a substantially different menu.
10. If previous cycles are unavailable, independently create a diverse week and label it **Rotating Meal Cycle**.
